FirstSeen · an agent that catches jobs the minute they exist

detects new postings in under an hour · ~14,000 live roles · runs for < £2/month

Job boards show you postings hours or days after companies publish them. FirstSeen polls 88 employers' hiring systems directly every 30 minutes, works out each ad's UK visa sponsorship stance (rules first; Claude only for the genuinely ambiguous ones), checks the employer against the Home Office sponsor register, and publishes it all to a free site. It's a LangGraph agent exposed over MCP, every LLM call traced in Langfuse, deployed as a serverless cron with CI that polls live boards before any change ships. The site's stats are computed from the agent's own public audit log, so the numbers above age well: they're live.

send-rag · answers with receipts

faithfulness 0.799 · recall@5 0.645 → 0.710 · 4/4 trap questions refused

Ask a question about the UK's SEND Code of Practice and get an answer where every claim cites a document and a page. The part I care most about is the measurement loop: a 35-question golden set with corpus-verified ground truth showed that dense retrieval alone missed things statutory language makes obvious ("Section F", "20 weeks"), and that fusing in BM25 lifted recall@5 from 0.645 to 0.710. It also refused all four deliberately out-of-scope questions rather than hallucinate, which I'd argue is a grounded system's most underrated metric. Scored with ragas, judged by Claude, demoed in Streamlit.

pubmed-sectioner · a small model taken seriously

P99 200 ms · INT8: 4x smaller, zero accuracy loss · CI with container smoke tests

A deliberately modest model (DistilBERT, classifying sentences in medical abstracts) given the full production treatment most models never get: MLflow-tracked experiments where the transformer has to beat a TF-IDF baseline to earn its keep, INT8 quantisation, a monitored FastAPI service with Prometheus and Grafana, and a load test that produced a number I can quote with a straight face: 200 ms P99 at zero errors. The point of the project is the machinery; the model is almost incidental.
